Second Pass: a primer for instructors

What the close is, what a close memo is, what the drill trains, and how to fit it into one class meeting.

The financial close

At the end of each month a company closes its books. The accounting team records the month's last entries, reconciles the accounts and produces a ledger of balances that managers and auditors can rely on. Before anyone relies on those balances, someone has to explain the ones that moved.

The close memo

A close memo, which some firms call flux commentary, gives a sentence or two on why each account changed since last month. A staff accountant usually drafts it, and a manager or controller reads it before signing off on the month. More and more often an AI assistant writes the first draft from the ledger.

The memo only owes an explanation where the change is big enough to matter. The drill's case sets that rule plainly: a line owes commentary only when it clears both $25,000 and 10 percent.

What the student checks on each line. Do the figures in the sentence match the ledger? Does a document on file back the reason it gives? A memo can read well and still fail either test, which is why someone has to check it.

What Second Pass trains

Second Pass gives students repetitions on that review. On the public link a student first reads the ledger on its own and marks the lines worth a second look, before any of the memo appears. Then, line by line, the student makes a call. Let it stand means the memo's explanation holds up. Flag it means it has a problem.

A call alone is not enough. The student also has to name the reason, such as a figure that does not tie or a reason with nothing on file behind it, and the drill scores the call and the reason separately. The AI Native Accounting Foundation calls this kind of practice teach-back: the reviewer explains and defends the output instead of only approving it. Each answer then shows why the key says what it does, with the account's figures from the ledger.

A 35-minute lesson

  • 10 minutes before
    Set up the review

    Explain the close and the memo in your own words, or read the first two sections of this page aloud. Read the threshold rule from the case. Tell the class an AI assistant drafted the memo and that some lines are wrong, without saying which kinds of mistake to look for.

  • 15 minutes in the drill
    Each student works alone

    Students open the drill on a phone or laptop, read the ledger first, then go through the fourteen practice lines. On each one they make the call, tap the reason and lock it in. Five lines from a second company follow, scored on their own. Ask them not to compare answers until everyone is done.

  • 10 minutes of discussion
    Defend the calls

    Ask students to defend one flag and one stand out loud, naming the ledger figure or the document that settled it. Then work through a few of the questions below.

  1. Which figure in the memo did not match the ledger?
  2. Which reason sounded specific but had nothing on file behind it?
  3. Which account needed an explanation and got none?
  4. Which clean line did you flag, and what made you doubt it?

The facilitator guide has a longer session plan with the answer to each line, and the review protocol is the same check written as a procedure a reviewer follows at work.

Where it fits

Students who tested the drill placed it in auditing and intermediate accounting courses. One named professional skepticism as the skill it practices: testing a written explanation against the ledger and the documents on file before accepting it, as an auditor does.

So far eleven testers sent their drill logs, and 52 of their 64 calls matched the answer key. That is a small sample, and the review page sets out what it counts and what it does not.

A note on confidentiality

The checker page transmits nothing you paste into it. The ledger and the memo are read by the script in your own browser, and the only network request the page makes is for its font, which carries no ledger data. The drill sends nothing on its own either. On the public link a round reaches the results form only if the student presses Send my results on the end screen, and the tester link never posts at all. If you build a case from your own ledger, use made-up figures or a close you have permission to use.
