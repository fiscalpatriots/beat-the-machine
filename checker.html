Second Pass Checker

Paste a ledger and the memo somebody drafted about it. The checker recomputes every line, ties each sentence to an account, and tests the arithmetic, the direction and the silence. What is left over is the part that needs a person, and it prints as a short list of questions.

Nothing you paste leaves this page. The checker is one HTML file with no libraries, no network calls and no storage. Close the tab and the ledger is gone.

Tab separated, comma separated or column aligned. Dollar signs, commas and (parentheses) for negatives are all read.

Free text. Lines that start with a label such as S1., 1. or (a) are kept whole; anything else is split into sentences. Numbering, bullets and Word's curly quotes are stripped.

Account numbers only, with plus, minus, times, divide and parentheses. A percent in the memo that is not a line percent is tested against every ratio, for both months and for the change between them.

The two prompts

The checker sits between them. The first one drafts the memo in whichever AI tool the firm already uses; the second one is run only on the sentences that survived the mechanical checks. Prompt 1 is written in a fixed shape on purpose. A numbered line that carries the account number, the prior and current figures exactly as the ledger prints them, and one direction word, ties to a ledger line on the strongest tier of the match, so a memo drafted this way does not land in Unmatched, review by hand.

Prompt 1, draft the memo

Use it before the memo exists

Hands your ledger and your threshold to the drafting tool and fixes the shape of every line: account number, prior, current, change, percent, direction, one reason and the source that reason came from. Lines below the threshold get one closing sentence and nothing else.

Prompt 2, second pass

Use it after a run, on what survived

Hands over the ledger, the memo and only the sentences that passed the arithmetic and the direction, with the two judgment questions and an instruction not to recompute what the checker already computed. Run the checker first, or this one has nothing to carry.

How to use it in a close

Run it first, before anybody reads the memo. The four mechanical checks settle what can be settled from the numbers, and the reviewer's time then goes only to the queue at the bottom: whether the driver is supported by something on file, and whether the period is right. Those two need a person.
