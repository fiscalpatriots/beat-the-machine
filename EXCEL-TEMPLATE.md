# The Excel edition

`Second-Pass-Excel-Template.xlsx` is the Second Pass review run in the tool a controller already
has open. The browser checker parses memo sentences; a spreadsheet cannot, and this workbook does
not pretend otherwise. What it does is recompute the ledger, decide which lines owe commentary,
name the lines nobody explained, and lay the ledger figure beside the figure the sentence states
so the reviewer settles the arithmetic and the direction in one glance.

Version 1.0, built 13 September 2026 by Khaled Alkurd. It is generated by
`build_excel_template.py` in this repository, so a correction is a change to the script and a
rebuild rather than a hand edit nobody can reproduce.

## The six sheets

| Sheet | What is on it |
| --- | --- |
| Read me | What the workbook does and does not do, the five steps, the privacy line, the version and the link to the site. |
| Ledger | Account, Name, Prior, Current, then Change, Percent, Clears dollar leg, Clears percent leg and Owes commentary as formulas against the Settings cells. The fourteen Halyard accounts are prefilled and thirty blank rows below them carry the same formulas. |
| Settings | Dollar floor $25,000, percent floor 10, the rule as a dropdown reading both or either, close period, memo version, and the two tie tolerances the Memo sheet uses. |
| Memo | One sentence per row. Account comes from a dropdown reading the Ledger, the reviewer types the figure the sentence states and picks which ledger figure it claims to be (Prior, Current, Change or Percent), and the sheet looks the ledger figure up, tests the tie, reads the direction off the sign of the change, and returns FAIL, INFO or REVIEW. The twelve Halyard sentences are prefilled and matched, with thirty blank rows below. |
| Silence | Every ledger line with the count of sentences written against it, from COUNTIF over the Memo accounts, and a status. A line that owes commentary and has no sentence reads **Owes an explanation, none written**, and the count of those sits at the top. |
| Reviewer log | The fourteen columns of `EVIDENCE-LOG-TEMPLATE.csv` in the same order, with dropdowns on check, result and resolved, and the three worked example rows. |

Mason green headers, gold on the Result and status columns, thousands separators with negatives in
parentheses, percent to a tenth, frozen header rows, set column widths, filters and a print area on
every sheet.

## What it does

- **Threshold.** Change and percent recomputed from the two balances alone, each leg tested against
  its floor, and the rule from Settings applied. Both legs or either leg, and changing that one cell
  reprices every line.
- **Silence.** Every account that clears the rule and carries no sentence, named.
- **Arithmetic and direction, laid out for the reviewer.** The workbook does not read the sentence.
  It puts the figure the reviewer typed beside the ledger figure the reviewer says it claims to be,
  compares them within a tolerance, and compares the direction the reviewer read out of the sentence
  against the sign of the movement. The judgment of what the sentence claims is the reviewer's; the
  comparison is the workbook's.
- **Result.** FAIL if either tie comes back no. INFO if the account owes no commentary, because a
  sentence that owes nothing can still say something false. REVIEW otherwise, which is the queue a
  person reads.

## What it does not do

- It does not parse English. No sentence is scanned for figures, accounts or direction words, and a
  row is only as good as the account, figure and basis the reviewer picked.
- It does not judge a driver or a period. Those two stay with the reviewer, as in `PROTOCOL.md`.
- It does not detect a contradiction. S1 and S10 both land on account 6200 and both pass.
- It does not decide. Nothing in the file says a memo is fit to release; a reviewer signs the log.
- Nothing leaves the file. No links to other workbooks, no web queries, no macros.

## Test results, 13 September 2026

Opened with openpyxl and recomputed in Python against the same rules the formulas encode. Twenty
three checks, no failures.

- Every computed cell on Ledger (220), Memo (252) and Silence (308) holds a formula.
- Settings reads $25,000, 10 percent and both. The Reviewer log carries the fourteen CSV columns in
  order and the three example rows. The dropdowns are present on the account, basis, direction,
  check, result and resolved columns.
- Seven of the fourteen accounts owe commentary: 4100, 4200, 5100, 6000, 6200, 6400 and 7100.
- The twelve sentences come back S1 REVIEW, **S2 FAIL**, S3 INFO, S4 INFO, S5 REVIEW, **S6 FAIL**,
  S7 REVIEW, S8 INFO, S9 REVIEW, S10 REVIEW, S11 INFO, S12 INFO.
  - S2 fails on the figure: the memo says $42,000 and account 6400 moved $47,000.
  - S6 fails on direction: the memo says declined and account 5100 rose $73,800.
  - S3, S8 and S12 read INFO because 6300, 6100 and 6500 clear neither leg or fail the dollar leg,
    and S4 and S11 read INFO for the same reason on 4000 and 5000, where the dollars are large and
    the percent is under ten.
- Silence lists exactly two accounts: **6000 Warehouse wages**, which moved $72,500 and 11.5 percent
  with nothing written against it, and 4200 Freight billed to customers, which the twelve sentences
  never mention. The S0 sentence that carries 4200 in the game sits on card one rather than in the
  memo block, so its absence here is correct.

**One caveat, and it is worth stating plainly.** openpyxl writes formulas but does not compute them,
and no copy of Excel ran against this file. Every computed cell therefore holds a formula and no
cached answer, and Excel calculates the whole workbook the moment it opens. The expected results
above were recomputed in Python from the prefilled inputs against the same rules, not read out of
Excel. Anyone taking this into a close should open it once, confirm the Result column paints FAIL on
S2 and S6 and the Silence sheet names 6000 and 4200, and only then replace the Halyard rows.
