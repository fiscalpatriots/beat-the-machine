# The Excel edition

`Second-Pass-Excel-Template.xlsx` is the Second Pass review run in the tool a controller already
has open. The browser checker parses memo sentences; a spreadsheet cannot, and this workbook does
not pretend otherwise. What it does is recompute the ledger, decide which lines owe commentary,
name the lines nobody explained, lay the ledger figure beside the figure a sentence states, and
count how much of the memo was settled and how much a person still owes an answer on.

Version 2.0, built 13 September 2026 by Khaled Alkurd, against `PROTOCOL.md` version 1.1 and the
checker contract in `CHECKER.md`. The prefilled case is `halyard-v4`. It is generated by
`build_excel_template.py` in this repository, so a correction is a change to the script and a
rebuild rather than a hand edit nobody can reproduce.

## The eight sheets

| Sheet | What is on it |
| --- | --- |
| Read me | What the workbook checks and what it does not check, both stated plainly, the six steps, the four statuses and why one is never promoted, the privacy line and the version. |
| Summary | Coverage by status, ledger coverage, the reviewer queue, the two judgment questions counted Yes, No and Not on file, two checks the sheet runs on itself, and the signature block. Every figure on it is a formula. |
| Ledger | Account, Name, Prior, Current, then Change, Percent, Clears dollar leg, Clears percent leg and Owes commentary as formulas against the Settings cells. The fourteen Halyard accounts are prefilled and thirty blank rows below them carry the same formulas. |
| Settings | Dollar floor $25,000, percent floor 10, the rule as a dropdown reading both or either, close period, memo version, and the two tie tolerances the Memo sheet uses. |
| Memo | One sentence per row, eighteen columns. The reviewer picks the account, types the figure the sentence states, picks which ledger figure it claims to be and picks the direction, and the sheet looks the figure up, tests the tie, reads the direction off the sign of the change, and suggests a status. The reviewer enters the status that stands, answers the two judgment questions Yes, No or Not on file, and writes the document relied on or the ask. Two more formulas flag the row for the queue and catch a promoted status. Twelve Halyard sentences are prefilled, thirty blank rows below. |
| Silence | Every ledger line with the count of sentences written against it, from COUNTIF over the Memo accounts, and a status that names a silent line, an account carrying two or more sentences, or a line that owes nothing. |
| Reviewer log | The fourteen columns of `EVIDENCE-LOG-TEMPLATE.csv` in the same order, with dropdowns on check, result and resolved, and four worked rows. |
| Change log | One row per release with what changed and why, in the shape `PROTOCOL.md` uses. A version used on a close is never edited. |

Mason green headers, gold on the columns the reviewer owns, thousands separators with negatives in
parentheses, percent to a tenth, frozen header rows, set column widths, filters, and a print area
on every one of the eight sheets.

## The four statuses

The statuses are the checker's and they carry the same meaning here.

| Status | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| Checked within scope | Every figure tested tied to the ledger. It does not mean the sentence is true. |
| Needs review | Something is unresolved and a person has to answer it. |
| Not checked | Nothing in the sentence could be tied to the ledger and tested, which is not the same as checked and found true. |
| Failed | A figure or a direction word disagrees with the ledger. |

Column L is the status the sheet suggests from the comparisons it ran. Column M is the reviewer's,
on a dropdown carrying those four and nothing else. Protocol step 3 says a status is never
promoted, so the sheet has to let a reviewer demote a sentence the arithmetic cleared and refuse
the reverse: column R compares the two against the severity order and prints yes where the entered
status is milder, and Summary counts those, which must be zero.

## The reviewer queue, counted rather than eyeballed

Protocol step 3 defines the queue as what the checks could not settle. Column Q marks a sentence
for it when the status is Failed, Needs review or Not checked, or when the account carries two or
more sentences. Summary adds the silent lines from the Silence sheet and prints the total. On the
prefilled Halyard case the queue is eight: seven sentences and one silent line.

## The two judgment questions

Columns N and O, each a dropdown reading Yes, No or Not on file, which is the answer set the
checker's reviewer queue uses on the page. Protocol step 4 says they are answered on the sentences
checked within scope, and on a needs-review sentence once it is resolved, so a sentence still
unresolved carries an ask in column P instead of an answer. Summary counts each answer and prints
how many sentences carry neither, and it flags any sentence checked within scope where both are
blank so the reason is written down in column P rather than left implied.

## What it does

- **Threshold.** Change and percent recomputed from the two balances alone, each leg tested against
  its floor, and the rule from Settings applied. Both legs or either leg, and changing that one cell
  reprices every line.
- **Silence.** Every account that clears the rule and carries no sentence, named and counted.
- **Arithmetic and direction, laid out for the reviewer.** The workbook does not read the sentence.
  It puts the figure the reviewer typed beside the ledger figure the reviewer says it claims to be,
  compares them within a tolerance, and compares the direction the reviewer read out of the sentence
  against the sign of the movement. The judgment of what the sentence claims is the reviewer's; the
  comparison is the workbook's.
- **Coverage.** Counts by status, not a pass rate, because a pass rate says nothing about how much
  of the memo was settled.

## What it does not do

- It does not parse English. No sentence is scanned for figures, accounts or direction words, and a
  row is only as good as the account, figure, basis and direction the reviewer picked.
- It does not judge a driver or a period. Those two stay with the reviewer, as in `PROTOCOL.md`.
- It does not detect a contradiction between two sentences. S6 and S10 both land on account 4000 and
  both tie, and all the sheet can do is count them and send the account to the queue.
- It does not decide. Nothing in the file says a memo is fit to release; a reviewer signs the log.
- Nothing leaves the file. No links to other workbooks, no web queries, no macros.

## Test results, 13 September 2026

Opened with openpyxl and recomputed in Python against the same rules the formulas encode. Forty
one checks, no failures.

- Every computed cell on Ledger (220), Memo (336) and Silence (308) holds a formula, and Summary
  carries 31.
- The Reviewer log's fourteen headers match the first line of `EVIDENCE-LOG-TEMPLATE.csv` character
  for character. The dropdowns are present on account, basis, direction, status, both judgment
  questions, check, result and resolved. A print area is set on all eight sheets.
- Settings reads $25,000, 10 percent and both. Seven of the fourteen accounts owe commentary: 4100,
  4200, 5100, 6000, 6200, 6400 and 7100.
- The twelve sentences come back **S1 Failed**, S2 Needs review, **S3 Failed**, S4 Checked within
  scope, S5 Checked within scope, S6 Needs review, **S7 Failed**, S8 Checked within scope, S9
  Checked within scope, S10 Needs review, S11 Checked within scope, S12 Needs review.
  - S1 fails on the figure: the sentence says a decrease of $6,500 and account 4200 moved $65,000.
  - S3 fails on direction: the sentence says declined and account 5100 rose $73,800. Its figure
    ties, which is the point of keeping the two tests apart.
  - **S7 fails on the figure and stays open**: the sentence says $42,000 and account 6400 moved
    $47,000. The preparer amended it, the $5,000 above the named Coastal Grocers reserve is still
    unexplained and no June reserve rollforward is on file, so the Reviewer log row is filed
    resolved `no` with the controller sign-off pending.
  - S2, S6, S10 and S12 clear every mechanical test and the reviewer demoted each one: a program
    that is both finished and still running, two unsupported drivers on account 4000, and an
    interest driver pointing at a depreciation account that did not move. None of the twelve is
    promoted, which Summary checks.
- Coverage is five checked within scope, four needs review, none not checked and three failed.
- Silence lists exactly one account, **6000 Warehouse wages**, which moved $72,500 and 11.5 percent
  with nothing written against it. Account 4200 carried a silent line in version 1.0 and no longer
  does, because `halyard-v4` puts the freight sentence in the memo block.
- Account 4000 carries two sentences and goes to the queue on that alone. The queue totals eight.
- The driver question is answered Yes twice and Not on file once; the timing question Yes three
  times. The other nine sentences carry an ask in column P instead of an answer, which is what the
  protocol asks for on an unresolved sentence.

**One caveat, and it is worth stating plainly.** openpyxl writes formulas but does not compute them,
and no copy of Excel ran against this file. Every computed cell therefore holds a formula and no
cached answer, and Excel calculates the whole workbook the moment it opens. The expected results
above were recomputed in Python from the prefilled inputs against the same rules, not read out of
Excel. Anyone taking this into a close should open it once, confirm that Summary reads five, four,
zero and three, that the Silence sheet names 6000, and that the promoted-status count is zero, and
only then replace the Halyard rows.

## One note on numbering

`EVIDENCE-LOG-TEMPLATE.csv` was written against the earlier sentence numbering and calls the bad
debt sentence S2 and the Tri-State sentence S5. This workbook numbers in `halyard-v4` card order,
where they are S7 and S6. The columns are identical; only the line labels moved.
